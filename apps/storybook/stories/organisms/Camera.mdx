# Camera

Where you watch from. Grappling is hard to read from a bad angle: one body hides the other,
and the joint that matters is on the far side. The default camera is *directed* to solve
that.

## Directed

For each keyframe the camera picks an angle that:

1. keeps the attacked joint in sight (in a submission),
2. shows the two bodies side by side rather than one behind the other,
3. stays near the previous angle, so it does not jump around.

It glides from one angle to the next and orbits slowly while playing. In the site, grabbing
the view hands the camera over ("Free"), and a chip gives it back.

## Fixed angles

**Side** and **top** are fixed views around the pair. **Through the eyes** (`eyes0`,
`eyes1`) puts the camera in a fighter's head: what that person sees of the technique.

## In code

```ts
viewer.setCamera("directed"); // "free" | "side" | "top" | "eyes0" | "eyes1"
viewer.onGrab = () => { /* the person took the camera */ };
```

Cameras are not a registry yet: a new camera mode means a change to `viewer.ts`. See
`docs/library-plan.md`.
