# Attacked joint

In a submission, the attacked joint glows red, and the glow grows as the lock tightens.
This is the reason the project exists: to *see* where a technique puts its load.

<Canvas of={AttackedJoint.Armbar} />
<Controls of={AttackedJoint.Armbar} />

Scrub **frame** and open the **Reading** panel below the canvas: it shows the value the glow
paints (0 to 1), the measure behind it (here, the elbow's flexion in degrees) and whether
it is in the danger range.

## Where the number comes from

GrappleMap has no forces and no timing, so the load is read from geometry alone: how far
the joint is pushed through its range, gated by something of the opponent being where the
leverage comes from. For an armbar, the elbow must be nearly straight **and** the
opponent's hips must be on it. Each submission kind has one such metric, in
`@bjj3d/metrics`; see **Engine/Metrics**.

## Only one joint lights

The viewer lights only the joint the technique attacks, chosen from the transition's tags
(an `armbar` tag attacks an elbow; which elbow is whichever reads highest over the clip).
Measuring every joint all the time would light joints that nothing attacks: arms are
often straight, necks often bent. `docs/validation.md` counts how often that would happen.

## On screen

The glow is part of the materials (every look picks it up). The site also draws a ring
around the joint while the lock is on, and at the first frame in the danger range slows
time and pushes the camera in: the finishing moment.
