# Mannequin

One fighter: a wooden figure, as an artist's mannequin. An egg head, a short neck with a
collar seam, a chest with shoulders, a barrel abdomen, a brief-shaped pelvis, deltoid upper
arms, tapered forearms, thighs and shins, shoe-like feet, and a belt. Each piece's width and
depth were measured from five views of a reference figure (`docs/character.html`); its
length is GrappleMap's bone.

A ball fills every joint, a little smaller than the ends it joins: straight, it shows as the
seam; bent, it closes the gap. The waist has a filler too, and the pelvis and the abdomen lean
half-way to the line from mid-hip to neck, so a straight back is straight even though
GrappleMap's Core sits behind it. The hand has two digits: four fingers folding together at
Hand, by the angle GrappleMap stores, and a thumb with one fold that follows them (GrappleMap
has no thumb).

## Nothing deforms

Every piece is placed straight from the 23 joints, every frame. There is no skin, no
skinning and no rig: the figure *is* the data. If a limb looks wrong, the pose is wrong,
and that is something you want to see.

## Thickness and the contact solver

GrappleMap's figures are stick figures. Given thickness, two of them would sink into each
other. The contact solver (`@bjj3d/core/contact`, sizes in `data/body/capsules.json`) keeps
capsules apart that are close to the pieces; the wooden figure is a little fuller than them in
the chest (13.8 cm half depth against 9.9) and the thighs (8.7 against 6.9), so in tight
contact the wood may touch a little more than the solver sees.

**Build** scales the pieces from slim (0.8) to sturdy (1.2). The solver is tuned for the
standard build (1), so a much sturdier fighter may overlap a little more.
