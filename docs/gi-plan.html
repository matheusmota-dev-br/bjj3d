The gi and the belt as part of the pose

GrappleMap stores two skeletons of 23 joints and nothing else, which is enough for no-gi. In gi jiu-jitsu the cloth is part of the technique: a lapel pulled out from under the belt and fed around a leg, a collar opened for a choke, a belt held to control the back. This page shows how the gi becomes a second layer of state, frame by frame, without changing the skeleton.

1. Two layers over real positions

Positions and one transition from GrappleMap.txt, put through the repository's contact solver (packages/core/src/contact.ts) with its capsules thickened by the gi, as in the plan. The gi@1 points are draped in this page, and the fabric is built from them: each jacket panel's front edge runs through its lapel chain, the skirt's bottom through BackHem, each sleeve ends at its cuff, the belt's tails follow their chains. The points are the cloth's handles, the way bones are a skin's. Pull a point with the grip slider and the panel comes with it. Hover a point to see its name. The fighters are the site's wooden mannequin (packages/three/src/mannequin.ts), and the gi is laid on its pieces.

The 3D preview needs WebGL and access to cdn.jsdelivr.net.
Everything else on this page works without it.

    The jacket follows the spine (MidHip → Core → Neck), the belt cinches it at the waist, and no vertex of any piece is left inside a piece of either mannequin: the other fighter's hands, knees and feet press the cloth instead of passing through it. Sleeves and pants are laid on the mannequin's own limbs, ring by ring in each piece's frame, with a fan of rings turning about the elbow and the knee: the outside of a bend always has a joint's width of fabric over it and the inside folds, so no bend opens the cloth onto the body. The fabric is procedural (no modelled garment yet) and has no wrinkles; an authored gi mesh would bind to the same points.

    2. gi@1: free points and anchored regions

    The rule that keeps the new degrees of freedom small: a free point only where the cloth can leave the body. Where the cloth follows the body (sleeve, pants, belt band), nothing is stored. A grip there names a place on a bone, and that place moves with the skeleton.

    The 29 points, per player

    • Nape: the back of the collar, where both lapels start.
    • LeftLapel ×7: 0 neck side, 1 collarbone, 2 upper chest, 3 sternum, 4 lower chest, 5 at the belt, 6 skirt corner. The left panel crosses over the right one.
    • RightLapel ×7: the same edge on the right panel, under the left one.
    • BackHem ×3, the cuffs ×4, BeltKnot, two BeltTails ×3.

    Anchored regions (no stored points)

    3. Relations: what positions cannot say

    At this resolution, seven points cannot tell whether a lapel goes behind a leg or in front of it. So topology is stored explicitly, over a range of keyframes, and the validator checks it against the positions.

    grip

    A hand holds a target. kind (fingers-in, thumb-in, pistol, pocket, pinch) is optional. Checked: the hand within 6 cm of the target.

    wrap

    A stretch of a chain goes around a bone, turns times (signed). Checked: the winding number of the stretch, projected across the bone, within ±0.25.

    untucked (state)

    Whether the belt still holds a lapel. Tucked, point 5 stays at the belt band (4 cm). Untucked, the lapel can be pulled out and fed.

    pin

    Cloth trapped under a body part, against the mat or the other body. Checked: each point of the stretch within 2 cm of that bone's capsule.

    A lapel feed needs no new relation: pulled out, under the arm, back to the other hand is untucked + wrap + grip.

    A cross collar choke from closed guard. Player 1 is the bottom player in GrappleMap's convention. The steps are illustrative; the real frames are made in the editor.

    6. Drape and build

    Physics is a tool here, and the stored points are what counts. A cloth simulation as the source of truth could be neither reviewed nor reproduced.

    Rest drape

    The points laid on the torso's frame (Neck, MidHip, the shoulder line), then a fixed number of position-based steps: gravity on hanging points, no segment longer than rest, nothing inside the mannequin or under the mat. Deterministic, so it is what this page runs.

    In-between frames

    Densifying interpolates gi points linearly, then projects the same constraints, with that frame range's relations as hard constraints: a gripped point stays at the hand.

    The fabric follows the points

    The drawn gi is a mesh built from the skeleton, with the gi points as its handles: each jacket panel's front edge runs through its lapel chain, the skirt's bottom through BackHem, each sleeve ends at its cuff, the belt's tails follow their chains. An authored garment would bind to the same points, as a skin binds to bones.

    Cloth never moves the body

    In gi@1, pulling a lapel does not drag the opponent. Tension is a reading, not a force. Cloth can bunch but not stretch: at most 105% of rest length.

    wrap: winding number around the boneturns ± 0.25
    pin: stretch at the bone's capsule2 cm
    No gi point inside a capsule1 cm, as the solver's OVERLAP
    Relations name existing chains, points and framesexact

    Collar chokes and the Ezekiel become metrics: two collar grips (or one grip and the Nape) form a loop around the neck, and the reading is how much shorter the loop is than at rest. Calibrated like the others in docs/validation.md.

    8. Appearance: gi colour, belt, degrees

    Appearance is the fighter's option, not the block's: the same transition can be watched in any gi. Today every mannequin wears a belt; with attire, the belt belongs to the gi, and no-gi fighters have none.

    Coloured belts: 0 to 4 white stripes on a black bar. Black belt: a red bar, 0 to 6 degrees. Kids' ranks show that ranks are a registry, like BELT_COLORS today. The choice also paints the 3D preview.

    9. Packages

    • @bjj3d/attire (new): the registry, gi@1 (points, chains, regions, rest lengths, drape), constraint projection, target resolution, winding number. Pure math on Vec3, no three.js.
    • three: the gi mesh, driven by the points; the belt with degrees on its tail.
    • data: block.schema.json, ids.json, the build merging blocks with GrappleMap.
    • editor: moving chain points, untucking, adding and ending relations.
    • metrics: collar-choke and Ezekiel readings.

    10. Phases and open decisions

    1. Blocks and ids. bjj3d/block@1 without attire, ids.json for GrappleMap, techniques reference ids. Nothing visible changes.
    2. gi@1 visible. @bjj3d/attire with the rest drape, drawn by three, belt degrees, gi as a fighter option. Every GrappleMap transition can be watched in a gi.
    3. Editor. Move gi points, untuck, add grips; grip and length rules in validate.
    4. Wraps and pins. The relations, the winding number, the cloth projection between keyframes.
    5. First gi techniques. Cross collar from closed guard, Ezekiel from mount, bow and arrow, a lapel feed.
    6. Metrics. Collar chokes and Ezekiel, calibrated, in docs/validation.md.
    Open decisionNotes
    Points per lapelSeven covers collar, lapel and skirt grips; a worm guard wrap may want more. gi@1 is versioned, so gi@2 can add points.
    Lapel overlap under mirrorA mirrored gi is right over left. Accept it, or store the overlap as state and keep it.
    Body sizeRest lengths scale with the torso; whether one gi@1 fits all GrappleMap figures needs measuring.
    Feet on clothSpider guard, De la Riva with a sleeve: a foot grip, or a push relation?
